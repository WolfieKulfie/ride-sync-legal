The big blue one is better at navigation. Obviously. It has more engineers than I have users. What it will not do is hold six riders together on a bad road, or keep drawing your line after the signal gives up. So I went and built the parts nobody else could be bothered with.

Everyone on one map, with the gap in kilometres.

Every rider is a disc carrying their name and how far away they are. From the back you can see exactly how far ahead the pack has got, instead of doing that arithmetic yourself at dusk and getting it wrong.

Showswho is riding, who has stopped, and for how long
Tapany rider for directions straight to them

Send one code. That is the entire setup.

Name the ride, pick where you are going, share the code on WhatsApp. Everyone who taps it arrives on the same route with the meeting point and the time already filled in. Nothing to configure in a car park at seven in the morning.

Hostnow, or schedule it and everyone gets reminded
Sizeup to twelve riders on one code
Meetcheck in once you are within 150 m

Fuel, food, a photo stop. Said without stopping.

One tap and the whole group knows. Whoever is leading can call a break for everybody at once, so five riders do not independently decide to pull over in five different places.

The one button I would be delighted never to demonstrate.

Raise it and every screen in the ride turns red until somebody answers. They are told who and where, and can say they are on the way. With no signal it waits, then goes the second a bar comes back.

The line you rode, not the road it assumed you took.

Distance, moving time, average speed, climb, and the pins you dropped along the way. Send it to Strava or a Garmin, drop a card into the group chat, or take the file and never open this app again.

Download the ground before you lose the bars.

Karnataka is about 18 MB. Goa at street level is 75 MB. Take a whole state, or frame any part of the world and take only that. When the signal goes, the map switches to what you downloaded, on its own.
